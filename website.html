What Paluxy landowners should know about their water quality

Large-scale data center development has been proposed in this watershed. This guide — from TIAER researchers — is a science-based starting point focused on water quality and the long-term health of the Paluxy. TIAER isn't on any one side — the stake we care about is the one we all share: a healthy environment and a healthy economy. This guide helps you understand what's known and weigh the choices.

Two questions, answered plainly

Most people arrive with the same two questions. Here's the short version — the rest of this page goes deeper.

What's a watershed?

It's all the land that drains to a common waterway. Rain that falls across this area works its way — over the surface and underground — into the Paluxy River. What happens on the land upstream can affect the water everyone downstream depends on.

How does this affect me?

A large data center uses land and water, and changes how water moves across a site. The thing to watch isn't a single scary event — it's the long-term health and quality of the water in your watershed and your well. This guide helps you track it.

Water Quality Today

Where the Paluxy stands now

Water quality is TIAER's focus — and the baseline matters. Right now, TCEQ lists Segment 1229 as "Fully Supporting," meaning the river currently meets its water-quality standards for its designated uses. Establishing today's condition is what makes any future change visible.

Take Action

Three practical steps for landowners

Protecting water quality starts with documentation. A clear sequence — from establishing your baseline to engaging the public process.

01

Document your baseline

Before construction begins is the best time to test your water — surface and groundwater both. A documented pre-development record is your most durable reference point.

  • Register your well with Upper Trinity GCD
  • Ask about free / low-cost well testing
  • Note USGS Gage 08091500 for flow comparison
Find testing contacts →
02

Track the permit process

Any discharge to the Paluxy requires a TCEQ permit. Because Segment 1229 is "Fully Supporting," a Tier 2 Antidegradation review may apply — a public document.

  • Monitor TCEQ permit notices for this watershed
  • Review the antidegradation analysis
  • Note the public comment period dates
TCEQ Permitting →
03

Engage the process

The Texas system includes formal ways for affected landowners to participate. Written comments become part of the official record.

  • Submit written comment during the comment period
  • Understand individual vs. general permits
  • Connect with local watershed organizations
