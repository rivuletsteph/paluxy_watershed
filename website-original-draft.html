The Watershed

The Paluxy River system

The Paluxy drains approximately 740 square miles of north-central Texas before joining the Brazos near Glen Rose. TCEQ Segment 1229 covers the main stem through Somervell County — the reach most directly affected by proposed upstream development.

Path Forward

Three steps for Paluxy landowners

Water quality protection starts with documentation. Here is a clear, practical sequence — from establishing your baseline to engaging the regulatory process.

01

Document your baseline

Before construction begins is the most important time to test your water — surface and groundwater both. A documented pre-development record is your most durable scientific reference point.

  • Register your well with Upper Trinity GCD
  • Request free or low-cost well testing (confirm with GCD)
  • Document nearest surface water source
  • Note USGS Gage 08091500 for flow comparison
Find testing contacts →
02

Track the permit process

Any discharge to the Paluxy requires a TCEQ permit. Because Segment 1229 is currently "Fully Supporting," the developer must complete a Tier 2 Antidegradation Analysis — a public document.

  • Monitor TCEQ permit notices for this watershed
  • Obtain and review the Antidegradation Analysis
  • Understand what discharge volumes are proposed
  • Note the public comment period dates
TCEQ Permitting →
03

Engage the process

The Texas regulatory system includes formal mechanisms for affected landowners to participate. Written public comments become part of the official record; contested case hearings trigger evidentiary review.

  • Submit written comment during permit comment period
  • Request an Individual Permit (vs. General Permit)
  • Consider a contested case hearing request (SOAH)
  • Connect with Protect the Paluxy Valley, Inc.
